iSmile 2026 · Project plan

Backend plan:
registration, Psoola payments,
tickets by email, and the database

A plain-language plan for turning the iSmile website into a working registration system: people register, pay through Psoola (Visa, MasterCard, FIB, FastPay), receive their ticket by email, and every registration lands in a database that you can see inside your own admin.

Recommendation in one line: build our own small backend (PHP + MySQL) on the server you are buying, connected to Psoola's single API and webhook. Do not use Pretix for the 2026 summit — it can technically take Psoola only through a custom plugin on a self-hosted Pretix server, and it would put the attendee database outside your admin. Section 3 explains.

1. What we are building

Today the website has the whole visitor journey on screen — registration form, lunch options, payment choice, success page, sponsor form — but nothing behind it. The registration page shows a reference number that is made up in the browser, no money is taken, no email is sent, and no one's details are saved anywhere. Sponsor requests open WhatsApp or email.

The finished system does this, automatically, in all three languages:

1 · Visitor fills the form → 2 · Saved in the database → 3 · Pays on Psoola's page → 4 · Psoola tells our server (webhook) → 5 · Server confirms the payment → 6 · Ticket emailed with QR code → 7 · You see it all in the admin

Three rules run through every part of this plan:

  • No successful payment, no ticket. A ticket is created only after our server has confirmed with Psoola that the money arrived. Not when the visitor says so, not when the browser comes back from the payment page.
  • The server decides prices and status. The browser only shows them. Nobody can register for 0 IQD by editing the page.
  • Everything is in your admin. Registrations, student ID photos, payments, tickets, sponsor and booth requests — one login, one place, exportable to Excel.

2. Payments with Psoola

Who they are. Psoola is the payment company the organiser already works with. They combine several payment methods behind one merchant API and will give us that API together with a webhook. For iSmile it will take exactly four payment methods: Visa, MasterCard, FIB and FastPay — nothing else. For us this is the best possible shape: one contract, one API, one webhook, and the four methods our visitors actually use.

Two words you will hear a lot:

  • API — the address our server calls to say "create a payment of 150,000 IQD for reference ISM26-K7P2QX" and to ask "is payment 12345 paid?".
  • Webhook — the opposite direction: an address on our server that Psoola calls to say "payment 12345 succeeded" (or failed). This is how the ticket goes out without anyone watching a screen.

How one payment will work

#StepDetail
1Visitor finishes the form and presses PayOur server saves the registration as unpaid, works out the total itself (ticket + lunch days, from the prices in the admin), and asks Psoola to create a payment for that amount.
2Visitor is sent to Psoola's payment pageThey choose Visa or MasterCard, FIB, or FastPay there. Our site never sees card numbers or bank logins — Psoola handles that, which keeps us out of card-security rules.
3Visitor comes back to our siteThey see "Checking your payment…". This page never decides anything; it only waits for step 4.
4Psoola calls our webhookOur server checks the message really came from Psoola (signature or secret), then asks Psoola's API directly for the payment status rather than trusting the message alone.
5PaidRegistration becomes paid, a ticket number and QR code are created, the ticket email is queued. The waiting page turns into "You're in — check your email".
6Failed, cancelled or abandonedRegistration stays unpaid. No ticket. The visitor gets a "Pay now" link by email that starts a fresh payment for the same registration — they do not fill the form again.

Things that go wrong in real life, and what we do

  • The webhook arrives twice (this is normal for every payment provider). The second one is ignored: a payment can only be confirmed once, and a ticket is never made twice for the same registration.
  • The webhook arrives late or never. Every 10 minutes a small scheduled job asks Psoola about payments that are still "waiting" and closes them either way. A visitor whose bank was slow still gets the ticket.
  • The visitor closes the page mid-payment. Nothing is lost; their registration is unpaid and the "Pay now" email covers it.
  • Someone tries to pay 1,000 IQD for a 150,000 IQD ticket. Impossible: the amount is set by our server, and the confirmed amount from Psoola is compared with the expected one before the ticket is issued.
  • Psoola is down or answers with an error. The registration is already saved as unpaid. The visitor sees “We could not start the payment; we have saved your registration and will email you a payment link”, and the “Pay now” email goes out automatically once Psoola answers again. Nobody fills the form twice.
  • A price is changed in the admin while someone is paying. The amount is fixed at the moment the payment attempt is created and stored on that attempt. Psoola's confirmed amount must match that attempt, not the current price.
  • The same person registers twice. A second registration with the same email or phone is allowed (families share phones) but flagged in the admin, and an unpaid earlier one is offered in the “Pay now” email instead of a new charge.
  • The “Pay now” link. It contains a long random token, shows no personal details, expires after 7 days, and can be regenerated from the admin. A used token cannot start a second payment for a paid registration.
  • Capacity reached. When paid tickets reach the capacity limit, the form closes with a message; unpaid registrations do not hold a place. Lunch has its own limit per day for the caterer.
  • No seat-hold timers (as decided earlier): an unpaid registration simply stays unpaid.

3. Can Pretix help? — the answer

Pretix can be made to work with Psoola, but only on our own Pretix server with a plugin we write ourselves — and it does not give you the database inside your admin. For the 2026 summit it adds more work than it removes. Recommendation: not now.

The question was: can Pretix handle "one API plus a webhook, no ticket without a successful payment"? Here is the honest picture.

PointWhat we found
Does Pretix already support Psoola, FIB or FastPay?No Pretix ships Stripe, PayPal, SEPA, bank transfer and others — no Iraqi provider.
Can a new provider be added?Yes, as a plugin Pretix documents a "payment provider plugin" (a Python class that redirects to the gateway and confirms the payment). Psoola's redirect-and-webhook style fits that model.
Can we use that plugin on pretix.eu (their hosted service)?Not on the standard hosted plan pretix.eu's hosted service does not let customers upload their own plugins. A custom plugin means running Pretix on our own server (Python, PostgreSQL, Redis, a worker — a second, heavier system next to the website) or paying for their Enterprise arrangement. Confirm with sales@pretix.eu before ever choosing this route.
Kurdish language and right-to-left layoutKurdish is not available We would translate Pretix ourselves. Arabic exists; full right-to-left layout is uncertain. Our own site already does all three properly.
"The database inside my admin"No With Pretix, attendees live in Pretix's own admin. We would build screens that copy them into ours — extra work to end up where the custom backend starts.
Sponsors and exhibition boothsNot covered Pretix sells tickets; sponsor and booth requests need our own screens either way.
Time until 20 NovemberEight weeks Setting up a self-hosted Pretix, writing and testing a payment plugin, translating to Kurdish, then still building the admin screens does not fit.
What Pretix would give us for freeReal things A check-in phone app with QR scanning, a waiting list, invoices, and a battle-tested order system. Worth revisiting for iSmile 2027 if you want the check-in app — the database we build now can be exported into it.
Decision: our own backend, described in the rest of this plan. It is smaller than Pretix, it speaks Psoola directly, it keeps the three languages we already have, and it lives inside the admin you already use.

4. Tickets by email

When a payment is confirmed, the visitor receives one email within a minute. It contains:

  • Their name exactly as typed (it will also be the name on the certificate), the ticket type (Professional / Student), lunch days if bought, and the reference (ISM26-XXXXXX).
  • A QR code shown in the email and a PDF ticket attached, both carrying a unique ticket number. Scanning the QR at the door looks the ticket up in the database, so a screenshot cannot be used twice.
  • The venue, dates, and a link to the workshops page. In the visitor's chosen language.

Rules for sending

RuleMeaning
Never twiceEach ticket has a send status: pending → sent or failed. A sent ticket is not sent again by accident.
RetryA failed send is retried 3 times over an hour, then shows as failed in the admin.
Resend buttonIn the admin, next to each registration — for "I deleted the email" and "I typed my address wrong" (you fix the address, then resend).
Test modeUntil launch, every email goes to your own address, whatever address is on the form.
Failed email ≠ failed ticketIf the email cannot be delivered, the ticket still exists and is visible in the admin. The person can be found by name or phone at the door.

Which email service

Not a personal Gmail account. Gmail caps at roughly 500 emails a day, and a personal account sending tickets to two thousand strangers gets flagged as spam. Use a transactional email service — Brevo (free up to 300 emails/day, cheap above that) or Amazon SES — sending from tickets@ismile.krd on your own domain, with the domain records (SPF, DKIM) set so the emails land in the inbox, not in spam. The sending code is written as one swappable piece, so changing service later is an afternoon, not a rebuild.

Certificates of attendance (with the same name) are a separate, later feature: generated after the summit from the check-in list. Nothing in this plan blocks it.

5. The database

MySQL on your server. One row per registration, one per payment attempt, one per ticket, one per sponsor request — linked together so that from any registration you can see its payments, ticket and student check, and from any payment you can find the person. Nothing is deleted; things are marked instead, so there is always a history.

Privacy and keeping

  • Student ID photos are sensitive. They are visible only to admins with the "registration" role, and are deleted a fixed time after the summit (suggest 3 months) — the approval decision stays, the photo goes.
  • Daily automatic backup of the database and the photo folder to somewhere other than the server (the hosting's backup, or a cloud bucket). Test one restore before launch.
  • Exports (Excel / CSV) are logged with who exported and when.

7. Sponsors and exhibition booths — handle with care

You asked for care here, and it is deserved: a sponsor is worth many tickets, the amounts are large, and a mistake is public. The rule is simple: sponsorship and booths are never sold like tickets. They are requests that a person handles, with the database keeping the record.

StageWhat happens
NewThe sponsor form (already on the site) saves the request to the database instead of opening WhatsApp. The company gets an automatic "we received your request" email; you get a notification email.
ContactedSomeone on the team calls or emails. Notes are kept on the request. Assigned to a person so two people do not both call.
AgreedPackage, booth position and price agreed. An invoice is sent from the admin (a PDF with the iSmile details, a number and a due date).
PaidMarked by Finance when the money arrives (bank transfer, or an Psoola payment link if Psoola's limits allow the amount — confirm).
ConfirmedOnly now does the sponsor's logo appear on the website, from the existing sponsors list in the admin, and the booth spot is counted as taken.
Declined / withdrawnKept with a reason, so next year you know the history.

The mistakes this design prevents

  • Selling the same booth twice. Each tier has a number of spots (already in the admin). The admin shows spots taken vs available and refuses to confirm one more than exists.
  • Publishing a logo before the money arrives. A request cannot move to Confirmed without being Paid first (the Owner can override, and it is logged).
  • A company paying a ticket price by mistake. The sponsor form never leads to the ticket payment page. If a sponsor wants to attend, their staff register as normal attendees with normal tickets — or you issue complimentary tickets from the admin, marked as such.
  • Losing a request in a WhatsApp thread. Every request is in the list with a status and a date; the dashboard shows how many are open and how old the oldest is.

8. The server and the domain you are buying

You will buy these yourself. Here is exactly what to buy, and what to check before paying. The website today is plain files; the backend in this plan needs a place that can run code, hold a database, keep files private, and be reachable by Psoola's webhook at a fixed address.

Domain

  • The site already uses info@ismile.krd, so ismile.krd is the natural name; if it is not available, ismile.iq or ismile-summit.com. Buy it in the organiser's name with your own email as contact, for at least two years, and turn on auto-renew — an expired domain in November would take the whole summit offline.
  • You also get email addresses on it: info@, tickets@, sponsors@. The ticket emails will be sent "from" tickets@ismile.krd.

Server: two sensible choices

A · Managed web hosting with cPanel Recommended

Shared or "cloud" hosting with a control panel. PHP, MySQL, email, SSL certificate, daily backups and cron jobs are all ready; nobody has to maintain Linux.

Roughly $5–20 per month. Enough for this: a few thousand registrations and a few hundred simultaneous visitors is a small load.

Choose a plan that states: PHP 8.1 or newer, MySQL 8 / MariaDB 10.6+, free SSL (Let's Encrypt), cron jobs, at least 10 GB storage, daily backups kept for 30 days, SSH access.

B · A VPS (your own virtual server)

2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, 40 GB SSD, Ubuntu 24.04, with a panel (cPanel, Plesk or CloudPanel) installed on it. More control and room to grow.

Roughly $10–30 per month plus the panel licence if any.

Downside: someone must update it and watch it. Only pick this if that person exists. A VPS without a panel is not recommended for this team.

Either way: a provider with servers in Europe or the Gulf is fine; latency from Iraq is a few tens of milliseconds. Avoid free tiers and one-year promotional prices that jump later.

What we do with it in the first week

  1. Point the domain at the server; turn on HTTPS (free certificate). The address becomes https://ismile.krd.
  2. Upload the website. The existing server/admin.php replaces the GitHub-key saving, so the admin saves instantly on the server (this was already planned and is written; it has never run on real hosting, so expect one round of small fixes).
  3. Create the MySQL database and the private folder for ID photos (outside the public web folder).
  4. Set up a staging copy at test.ismile.krd with Psoola's test credentials, so every feature is tried there before it touches the real site.
  5. Give Psoola the webhook address (https://ismile.krd/api/psoola-webhook) — they need it to send confirmations.

9. Technology choice

PHP 8 + MySQL, on the hosting above, keeping the current website exactly as it is. Reasons, in order:

  • It is what managed hosting runs natively — no extra services, no server administrator needed.
  • The admin's server half (server/admin.php) is already PHP; the new parts sit next to it and share its login.
  • The front end (forms, three languages, right-to-left, light/dark themes) stays. Each form gains one "send to server" call; nothing is rebuilt.
  • Any local developer can maintain PHP + MySQL later.

The 23 September plan proposed Django + PostgreSQL + Redis + Pretix. That was right for a larger team and a longer runway; with Psoola giving one API, a managed server, and eight weeks, it is more machinery than the job needs. If the summit grows to tens of thousands of attendees, the database designed in section 5 moves to a bigger stack without changing what it stores.

10. Security essentials

  • HTTPS everywhere; the admin refuses plain HTTP.
  • Psoola secrets, database password and email keys live in one file on the server outside the web folder — never in the website files, never in GitHub.
  • Every form field is checked again on the server (length, format, allowed values, file type by content not by name, 8 MB limit).
  • Rate limits on the registration form, the "Pay now" link and the admin login; admin login locks for 15 minutes after 5 wrong tries; the Owner account gets two-factor codes.
  • Webhook: signature verified, then the status re-fetched from Psoola's API; unknown or unsigned calls are logged and ignored.
  • ID photos and exports are never at a public address; they are served through the admin's login.
  • The usual web protections, in plain words: the database is only ever queried with prepared statements (no typed-in text can become a command); every admin action carries a one-time form token so a link in an email cannot trigger it; everything shown on screen is escaped so a name typed as code stays text; admin sessions end after 12 hours or 30 minutes idle and cookies are HTTPS-only.
  • Logs never contain passwords, card details or full ID photos.
  • Monitoring: a free uptime check (e.g. UptimeRobot) pings the site every 5 minutes and messages you if it is down; the server emails the “something needs attention” alert (section 4) for failed webhooks, failed emails, missed backups and low disk. The goal is that you hear about a problem before a visitor does.
  • Privacy notice and consent: a short notice on the form says what we collect, why (ticket, certificate, student check), how long we keep it, and who to contact. Students tick a box agreeing to the ID check. The wording is in all three languages and editable in the admin.
